How to use the word counter

Type or paste text into the box and the counts update immediately: words, characters with and without spaces, sentences, paragraphs and lines. Below the counts you will find an estimated reading time and speaking time, a check against common character limits, and a list of the words you repeat most.

The buttons above the text change its case or remove extra spaces. If you apply one by accident, Undo restores the previous text. Nothing you type is stored or sent anywhere.

How the counts are calculated

  • Words are groups of characters separated by spaces or line breaks. A group counts only if it contains a letter or digit, so a lone dash does not count. Hyphenated words such as well-known and addresses such as name@example.com count as one word, which matches how Google Docs counts. Chinese, Japanese and Thai text, which has no spaces between words, is split using your browser's word-boundary rules.
  • Characters count what you see. An emoji, or a letter with an accent, counts as one character even when it is stored as several code points. No spaces leaves out spaces, tabs and line breaks.
  • Sentences are detected with your browser's sentence rules, which handle most abbreviations such as Dr. correctly. Very informal text may be miscounted.
  • Paragraphs are the non-empty lines of text, so every press of Enter starts a new paragraph, as it does in a word processor. Lines counts every line, including blank ones.

Other tools can report slightly different totals. Microsoft Word, Google Docs and this page each make small choices about hyphens, slashes and symbols. If you must meet an exact requirement, such as an essay limit, check with the tool the recipient will use.

Reading time and speaking time

Reading time assumes 238 words per minute, the average silent reading speed for adults reading non-fiction found in a 2019 review of reading studies. Speaking time assumes 130 words per minute, a comfortable pace for a presentation or voice-over. Your own speed will vary with the material and the audience, so treat both as estimates.

Tips for writing to a length

  • Write first and count afterwards. Cutting a draft that is a little long usually improves it.
  • Check the most repeated words list. A word that appears far more often than you meant to use it is a sign to vary your wording or tighten a paragraph.
  • For web copy, aim for short paragraphs. Two or three sentences are easier to scan on a phone.
  • Do not write to a target keyword percentage. Search engines do not use a fixed keyword density, and repeating a phrase to hit one makes text harder to read.

Frequently asked questions

Does the word counter save or upload my text?

No. Counting happens in your browser and the text is not stored or transmitted. Closing or reloading the page clears it.

Does it count spaces as characters?

The Characters figure includes spaces and line breaks. The No spaces figure leaves them out, so you can use whichever your requirement specifies.

Why is my count different from Microsoft Word?

Each program decides slightly differently how to treat hyphens, slashes, numbers and standalone symbols. A difference of a few words in a long document is normal.

Does it work for languages other than English?

Yes. Languages that use spaces between words, including Spanish, French, German, Hindi and Korean, are counted by spaces. For Chinese, Japanese and Thai your browser's word segmentation is used. The list of common words to ignore is English only.

How is reading time calculated?

It divides the word count by 238 words per minute. Dense technical writing takes longer to read, and light fiction may be quicker.
